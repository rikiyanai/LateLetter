Source: Children's Behavior toward and Understanding of Robotic and Living Dogs
Authors: Gail F. Melson et al.
Journal: Journal of Applied Developmental Psychology 30(2), 2009
Record: https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ830357
DOI page: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0193397308001329
Accessed: 2026-07-30
Type: Peer-reviewed empirical study

Relevant findings
-----------------

The study compared children's interaction with Sony AIBO and a living dog.
Children distinguished the living dog in several judgments and physical
behaviors, yet many still attributed mental states, sociality and moral standing
to AIBO and interacted with it socially. Autonomous embodied behavior can
therefore support meaningful social interpretation without erasing the
artifact/living-animal distinction.

LateLetter transfer
-------------------

Consistent autonomous behavior, recognition and memory deserve ethical care.
The product should clearly remain a designed virtual animal and avoid claims of
sentience or genuine emotion.

Limit
-----

This study involved 72 children and a physical robot. It does not prove the
same response for adults, grief contexts, text renderers or LateLetter.
